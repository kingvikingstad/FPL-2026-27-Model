## 4. What GW1 does and does not license

**Does not:** mark down Man United or Palace for the two inversions.
`studies/promoted_upset.py` tested exactly this over 31 seasons — β −0.044 ppg, p=0.263,
with a matching mid-season placebo. One week of finishing variance is not evidence about
a team's rate.

**Does:** flag that the model still cannot see any of this. There is **no in-season update
path** — `TeamModel.fit()` reads the 25/26 reconstruction, `two_season_evidence()` pools
24/25+25/26, and `build_pms` is 2025-26 only. The GW2 board (regenerated 24 Aug 07:07) has
Man United third by team total (45.7) and Brighton fourteenth (29.3), which is a pre-season
prior, not a posterior. Prices, ownership and flags refresh; priors do not.

`docs/GW1_SCORING_2026-08-26.md` now scores the complete round (r 0.530, rho 0.677, MAE 1.59
against 2.03 for a constant and 2.52 for price, n=577), so the numbers to work from are
those, not the 22 Aug interim. Its §2 is the one that bears on this review: conditional on
`minutes > 0` the model is **essentially unbiased (−0.04)**, and the whole +0.48 full-sample
over-projection is the availability layer — ~276 points assigned to players who never took
the field. That is the pre-registration target. The minutes update is the one worth
building; the rate update is the one to leave alone.

Two things in this review are consistent with that and worth stating as such. Porro's flag
resolving after 164k transfers out (§3) is the same availability volatility measured from
the manager side. And the per-club residual ordering in that doc's §4 — strong clubs under,
Hull/Brighton/Leeds over — is tagged `[CHECK]`, not a finding; nothing in §5 below prices it.
