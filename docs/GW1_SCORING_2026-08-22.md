The model beats both naive baselines on every metric it shares with them — 1.59 MAE
against 2.03 for a constant and 2.48 for price. Rank agreement (rho 0.68) is
substantially better than level agreement (r 0.51), which is the expected shape for a
single week: ordering players is tractable, calling the exact scoreline is not.

**[VERIFIED]** on n=345, but one gameweek and provisional bonus. This is a sanity result,
not a validated skill estimate.

## 2. The diagnostic that matters

Restricted to players who actually appeared (`minutes > 0`, n=178):

| | r | rho | MAE | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| model `mean` | 0.263 | 0.365 | 2.10 | −0.15 |

Correlation roughly halves and MAE rises above the constant baseline. **Most of the
model's measured skill in GW1 came from predicting who plays, not how well they play.**

That is not a defect — it is the project's own central claim, confirmed on real data for
the first time. PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE §3: "minutes/availability is the dominant, most-resolvable
single-GW lever (~32% of variance), and the single largest validated gain in the project."
The corollary is equally important and equally expected: conditional on playing, one week
of returns is mostly noise, and no amount of xG modelling fixes that at n=1.

The two tails are one match: **Hull 2-0 Man United**. The model had Man United third on
attack (blended Elo 1830) and Hull last (1447), and the result inverted it.

**This is not evidence the team layer is wrong.** A Poisson model that never let Hull win
that fixture would be a worse model, not a better one. Recording it because a run of such
misses *would* be evidence, and the record has to start somewhere. Revisit at ~GW6, not now.

Two things sharpen that. First, the match itself: **Man United out-created Hull on xG,
1.81 to 1.26**, with 21 shots to 8 and 72% possession. Hull's xG was 94% set-play (1.19
of 1.26) against 0.07 in open play, and the xGOT split (Hull 1.72 on 1.26 xG, United 0.60
on 1.81) is a finishing swing, not a territorial one. On the quantity the model actually
estimates, its ordering held; the scoreline inverted it. The model's own margin was still
too wide — it implied λ 2.708 vs 0.868, a 3.1× edge, where realised xG was 1.4×.

Second, `studies/promoted_upset.py` tests whether an early defeat to a promoted club
predicts anything for the rest of the season, over 31 seasons. It does not: β −0.044 ppg,
p=0.263, and the mid-season placebo and the "any weak opponent" contrast land on the same
coefficient. **No club should be marked down for this result.**

## 4. The structural finding

**The model has no in-season update path.** Verified by inspection:

- `bayes_model.TeamModel.fit()` → `config.E0_RECON`, reconstructed from **25/26**
- `multiseason_priors.two_season_evidence()` → pools **24/25 + 25/26**
- `build_pms` → **2025-26** only
- `repo_events.preseason_minutes(season="2026-2027")` reads 26/27 but only friendlies,
  and its own docstring says they "are NOT competitive minutes and must not be treated
  as such"

So after GW1 the model still projects entirely from last season. Results can be scored
against it, but nothing flows back. Rebuilding the board on today's data refreshes prices,
ownership and availability — **not the team or player priors.**

This is now the highest-value open item, ahead of everything in §6 of PROJECT_KNOWLEDGE,
and it needs a decision rule fixed *before* results accumulate:

1. **Team layer.** Append 26/27 match λ to the E0 stack with a weight, or Bayesian-update
   the fitted posterior. The hyperparameter calibration (`revert` 0.963) was fitted on
   season-to-season reversion, not within-season updating — it does not transfer.
2. **Player minutes.** Realised starts are direct evidence on the dominant lever. A
   Beta update on `start_a`/`start_b` from actual appearances is the obvious move and
   the cheapest win, given §2.
3. **Player rates.** Weakest case. One match of xG against a Gamma prior built on two
   seasons will move almost nothing, and pretending otherwise invites overreaction.

Pre-register the weights before GW2 is scored, or this becomes curve-fitting on a
sample that is already visible.
