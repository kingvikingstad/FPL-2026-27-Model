Prediction scored: `predictions/gw1_board_locked_2026-08-21.csv`, saved before the

## 1. Headline

| | r | rho | MAE | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **model `mean`** | **0.530** | **0.677** | **1.59** | +0.48 |
| model `blended` (GW1 Solio blend) | 0.538 | 0.678 | 1.58 | +0.47 |
| baseline: price × 0.55 | 0.224 | 0.313 | 2.52 | +1.30 |
| baseline: everyone gets 2.0 | — | — | 2.03 | +0.45 |

**[VERIFIED]** The model beats both naive baselines on every shared metric: MAE 1.59
against 2.03 for a constant and 2.52 for price. Rank agreement (rho 0.68) again exceeds
level agreement (r 0.53), the expected shape for a single week — ordering players is
tractable, calling the scoreline is not.

Against the interim numbers, adding the remaining 4 fixtures moved r from 0.507 to 0.530
and left MAE unchanged at 1.59. Nothing in the headline was an artefact of the partial
sample.

## 2. The diagnostic that matters

Restricted to players who actually appeared (`minutes > 0`, n=296):

| | r | rho | MAE | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| model `mean` | 0.322 | 0.399 | 2.11 | −0.04 |

**[VERIFIED]** Correlation falls from 0.530 to 0.322 and MAE rises above the constant
baseline. Most of the model's measured skill in GW1 came from predicting who plays, not
how well they play — the project's own central claim (§3: minutes as the dominant,
most-resolvable lever), now confirmed on complete real data rather than a partial week.

The bias split is the sharper version of the same result and is new in the full sample:

- all players: **+0.48** per player
- conditional on appearing: **−0.04** per player

**[DERIVED]** The model's over-projection is almost entirely the availability layer.
Conditional on a player taking the field it is essentially unbiased; the whole +0.48
comes from projecting minutes for players who did not play. Across 577 players that is
**~276 points of projection that could never be scored**, and the per-club table below
is the same quantity redistributed.

## 3. Where it went wrong

**Availability — 13 players projected ≥3.0 played zero minutes, carrying 52.6 projected
points** (interim showed 8 and 33.8; the extra fixtures added more of the same failure):

|---|---|---|
| Amad | Man United | 6.18 |
| Sarr | Crystal Palace | 4.25 |
| Gyökeres | Arsenal | 4.23 |
| Diomande | Nott'm Forest | 4.12 |
| Mukiele | Sunderland | 3.93 |
| Palestra | Chelsea | 3.91 |
| O'Brien | Everton | 3.83 |
| Aït-Nouri | Man City | 3.79 |
| Watkins | Aston Villa | 3.76 |
| Yoro | Man United | 3.67 |

Given §2 this is the layer worth attacking, and it is the same conclusion the interim
reached — the full week did not change the diagnosis, it enlarged the evidence.

**The reverse error barely exists.** Exactly one player projected ≤1.5 scored 8+
(Tzolakis, Hull, 0.79 → 10). The model is not missing hauls from nowhere; it is
awarding minutes to players who do not get them.

## 4. Per club — a hypothesis, not a finding

15 of 20 clubs came in under projection. The five that beat it are Hull (+34.6),
Brighton (+25.4), Leeds (+24.7), Everton (+18.3), Brentford (+9.2); the worst misses are
Man United (−73.5), Aston Villa (−37.6), Crystal Palace (−35.5), Tottenham (−33.2),
Man City (−31.4).

**[CHECK]** That ordering looks like the team-strength layer being too confident in
strong clubs and too harsh on weak ones — compression failure in the direction the
market would price. It is also exactly what one gameweek of noise looks like when you
sort clubs by residual after the fact. **Do not act on this.** It is a hypothesis to
pre-register and test over GW1–6, not a result. Note also that most of the per-club
deficit is the §2 availability bias landing club by club, so any team-layer test must
condition on appearance first or it will re-measure the minutes error.

## 5. Limitations

- **n=1 gameweek.** Nothing here is a skill estimate. Single-week FPL scores are
  dominated by variance in both directions.
- **[CHECK] The locked board joins on name+club, not `player_code`.** It predates
  `player_code` on the output, so this scoring violates the repo's hard join guard by
  necessity. 577 of ~584 projected players matched; the ~7 that did not are unaudited.
  Name+club is much safer than name alone, but future locked boards must carry
  `player_code` so this caveat disappears.
- Baseline `flat 2.0` has no correlation by construction (zero variance); only its MAE
  and bias are comparable.

## 6. What this licenses

Ticking §6.6 to "one week scored, model beats naive baselines, skill concentrated in
availability". Not to "validated". The next decision-relevant question is whether the
availability bias in §2 persists across GW2–6; if it does, it is a correctable
systematic error worth more than any refinement to the scoring layer.
